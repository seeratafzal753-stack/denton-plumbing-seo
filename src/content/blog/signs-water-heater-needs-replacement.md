A failing water heater rarely announces itself with a gentle heads-up. Often, homeowners only realize there is a problem when they step into a freezing shower or discover water pouring into their garage or utility room.

Being able to identify the subtle early warning signs of water heater failure can save you thousands of dollars in emergency flood restoration costs.

---

## 7 Warning Signs Your Water Heater is on Its Last Legs

### 1. The Unit is Over 10 Years Old
Check the manufacturer label on the side of your water heater for the serial number or manufacture date. In North Texas, where hard water accelerates internal corrosion, tanks operating beyond the 10-year mark are on borrowed time.

### 2. Rusty or Discolored Hot Water
If you turn on your hot water tap and see brownish or reddish water, the sacrificial anode rod inside your tank has likely dissolved, and the inner steel tank walls are now rusting from the inside out.

### 3. Rumbling, Popping, and Banging Noises
As hard water minerals bake onto the bottom of the tank, hardened sediment forms an insulating crust. When the burner turns on, steam bubbles form under the crust, creating loud knocking and rumbling sounds like popcorn popping in a microwave.

### 4. Water Pooling Around the Tank Base
Even minor moisture or pooling around the bottom of the heater indicates a microscopic fracture in the tank lining. Once a tank fractures, catastrophic rupture is imminent.

### 5. Running Out of Hot Water Quickly
If your 50-gallon water heater used to supply two hot showers with ease and now runs lukewarm after 5 minutes, thick sediment has displaced the usable water volume, or one of the electric heating elements has burned out.

### 6. Constant Pilot Light or Thermocouple Failures
Gas heaters with aging thermocouples, faulty gas valves, or degraded draft venting will repeatedly shut off the pilot light, leading to inconsistent hot water.

### 7. Repeated Need for Costly Repairs
If you find yourself calling a plumber every few months to replace elements, thermostats, or valves on an older tank, the accumulated repair costs quickly exceed the price of installing a brand-new, energy-efficient model.
